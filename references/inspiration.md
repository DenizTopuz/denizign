# Denizign Inspiration

This document contains visual and interaction references for the Denizign portfolio.

Inspiration is directional.

Do not directly copy:
- layouts
- branding
- typography
- components
- illustrations
- copy

Use references to understand the desired level of quality, composition, storytelling and interaction.

The Denizign brand and design system always take priority.

**Sources of evidence.** Observations marked *(screenshot)* come from the supplied screenshots in `/references/inspiration/`. Observations marked *(notes)* come from Deniz's written notes, mainly for motion, which a still image cannot show. The reference URLs were not visited, so nothing here is based on live behaviour that was not described in the notes.

---

## Denizign Inspiration Synthesis

The references share one underlying idea: **typography and layout carry the design, and everything else is held back.** The sites differ in colour, mood and typeface, but the structure repeats. This matches the Denizign principle *clarity over decoration*.

### Typography

- **Scale contrast is the main device.** Headlines are very large and tightly set (Alex Graham, Catalin, Julian, Kristian, Re-Birth). Supporting copy is small and quiet (Julian, Alex Graham, Jonas). The contrast between the two does the hierarchy work, with little else.
- **Weight and tracking.** Display type is heavy with tight tracking in most references (Catalin, Julian, Alex Graham, Formula Vintage). Kristian is the exception, with a light serif.
- **Short headline, calm body.** Hero headlines are one or two ideas. Body text sits in a narrow column at a comfortable size (Jonas, Formula Vintage, Kristian), around the 55–72 character line length the design system already asks for.
- **Small labels as a third tier.** Tiny uppercase or tracked labels (Scope / Role / Client / Year, Based / Focus, section tags) organise information without adding visual weight (Kristian, Jonas, Alex Graham).
- **Selective emphasis.** One phrase in a headline is coloured, italicised or dimmed to direct attention (Kristian, Catalin, Alex Graham intro).

### Layout

- **Two-column compositions repeat.** A large title or label on one side, with explanatory content on the other (Jonas project and process, Formula Vintage overview, Kristian project overview).
- **Asymmetry over centring.** Content is usually left-aligned or placed on an offset grid. Julian's centred hero is the exception, and it works only because there is almost no content.
- **Hairline structure.** Thin dividers form tables and lists (Jonas spec table, Alex Graham services list, Kristian project facts). They replace boxes and cards.
- **Large, unframed media.** Project images run wide with little chrome (Kristian, Formula Vintage, Julian).

### Hero

- **One dominant message**, with typography as the main visual (all hero screenshots).
- **Photography and type interact.** The portrait and the name overlap or share the composition (Alex Graham, Laura Gonzalez, Julian, Kristian).
- **Low content density.** A hero carries a headline, a few facts and a navigation bar, and not much else.
- **Movement is subtle and typographic.** Per the notes, first name and surname move horizontally (Alex Graham, Laura Gonzalez).
- **Small supporting facts** sit at the edges of the hero: location, role, focus (Kristian, Julian, Catalin, Laura Gonzalez).

### Selected work

- Large imagery leads, and the title and a short description come second (Alex Graham Projects).
- Explanatory copy is minimal, a few lines at most.
- Meta (year, type) is quiet and in a smaller tier.
- The hierarchy is image, then title, then one sentence, then tags.

### Case studies

- **Project hero.** A title with an immediate fact row (Kristian: Scope, Role, Client, Year), then a large image.
- **Overview.** A large statement or lead paragraph next to quieter supporting information (Formula Vintage, Kristian).
- **Context, challenge, process, solution.** Clearly separated sections, with a heading on one side and the story on the other (Jonas Process and Project).
- **Pacing.** Statement, fact, image, explanation. Large moments alternate with quiet ones.
- None of the references show outcomes or reflection clearly. Denizign's case-study structure already goes further here.

### Motion

- **Sequential reveals that follow reading order** (Re-Birth, Julian intro) *(notes, screenshot)*. In the screenshots, the words already read are bright and the rest are dimmed.
- **Typographic movement** such as a moving name (Alex Graham, Laura Gonzalez) *(notes)*.
- **Motion supports reading and storytelling**, and does not decorate it. This fits the Denizign motion principle *Fragment → Connect → Organise*.

### Imagery

- **Scale.** Imagery is large and often full width.
- **Photography is portrait-led and editorial** in the hero (Alex Graham, Laura Gonzalez, Kristian, Julian).
- **UI imagery** appears as the case-study hero (Kristian).
- Text and image work together in one composition, and the image is not just placed next to the text.

### Whitespace

- Whitespace separates narrative moments and sets the premium feel (Jonas, Formula Vintage, Julian intro).
- It makes the hierarchy readable without extra containers.
- Reading comfort comes from generous space around narrow text columns.

### Interaction

- Hover and scroll behaviour is mostly implied in the screenshots. A custom cursor and a floating "View" label appear (Alex Graham, Catalin, Kristian). These are decorative and are not recommended.
- Sequencing is the interaction pattern worth keeping: content is revealed in the order it should be read *(notes)*.

### Overall visual character

Editorial, confident, typographic and uncluttered. Big type, generous space, large imagery, hairline structure, little decoration. Most references are dark and several use strong colour. The structure and discipline are what Denizign should keep. The colour mood is not.

# References

## Reference 01 — Re-Birth

Name: Re-Birth

URL: https://re-birth.framer.website/#top

Screenshots:
- `re-birth.webp`

### What works

- Bold, highly readable typography with strong contrast between the highlighted and unhighlighted text *(screenshot)*.
- Easy to scan and restrained *(notes)*.
- During scrolling, words are highlighted one after another in the intended reading order *(notes)*.

### What Denizign can learn from it

- Sequential highlighting can guide reading when it follows the reading order.
- A single large statement on an otherwise empty screen is enough.

### What should NOT be copied

- The near-black background and the dimmed grey text. The dimmed words look low in contrast, which would fail the accessibility target if used for real content.
- The cursor dot.
- The exact text styling and typeface.

### Relevant areas

Typography, Motion, Storytelling (statement or positioning section).

## Reference 02 — Alex Graham (and Laura Gonzalez)

Name: Alex Graham portfolio template, with Laura Gonzalez as a related hero reference

URLs:
- https://td-alexfolio.framer.website/
- https://www.awwwards.com/sites/laura-gonzalez-ux-portfolio

Screenshots:
- `Hero-AlexGraham.webp`
- `Catalin-Hero.webp`
- `Hero-LauraGonzalez.webp`
- `WhatIDo-LauraGonzalez.webp`
- `Services-AlexGraham.webp`
- `Projects-AlexGraham.webp`
- `Overview-Section-Formula-AlexGraham.webp`
- `Intro-AlexGraham.webp` (not on the original list; see the final report)

### What works

- **Hero.** The name set very large across the whole viewport, interacting with a portrait. Per the notes, first name and surname move horizontally. The composition divides the viewport strongly without making the layout hard to read *(screenshot, notes)*.
- **Catalin hero.** A bold all-caps statement with small inline images, a short paragraph, and metadata in the corners *(screenshot)*.
- **What I do / Services.** Services are listed as a plain, structured list. A large section title is on one side and short text or a ruled list is on the other *(screenshot)*. The Alex Graham services list uses hairline dividers and numbering, which is very easy to scan.
- **Selected work.** One very large project image with a short description, a few tags and a quiet year *(screenshot)*.
- **Project overview.** A two-column layout with a big title on the left and a large lead paragraph on the right. The font sizes and content density feel comfortable *(screenshot, notes)*.
- **Intro.** A large statement in which one phrase is picked out in colour, followed by a smaller supporting paragraph and one secondary action *(screenshot)*.

### What Denizign can learn from it

- Use the name or a key statement as a visual element, with the portrait supporting it.
- Present capabilities as structured lists with dividers, instead of tiles or badges.
- Let one large project image carry a project preview.
- Use a large title on the left and a lead paragraph on the right for project overviews.
- Keep supporting information in the small label tier.

### What should NOT be copied

- Looping name animation. Brand motion rules avoid continuous looping animation; use a one-time or scroll-linked movement instead.
- Large text laid over a face, where the letters lose contrast and legibility.
- The glowing pill chips and the teal accent on the "what I do" screenshot (another accent colour and a glow effect).
- The mono font and the "//" label prefix (an extra typeface and a branded device).
- Whole sentences set in the accent colour (Intro screenshot). Red should stay under roughly 10% of the page.
- The rounded white project card, the circular red "View" cursor button, and the Framer or template badges.
- The outlined giant numeral, unless it proves useful in a service list.
- All-caps headlines (Catalin) and heavy pill-shaped inline images.

### Relevant areas

Hero, Selected work, Services / capabilities, Case studies, Typography, Layout, Motion.

- The pure black background.

# Recurring patterns across references

## 1. Oversized, tightly set display typography

- **What it is:** Very large headlines used as the main visual element.
- **Shown in:** Alex Graham, Laura Gonzalez, Catalin, Julian, Kristian, Formula Vintage, Re-Birth.
- **Why it is relevant:** Typography doing most of the work is a core Denizign principle, and the brand already defines a bold Manrope display tier.
- **Verdict:** **Adopt**, within the defined scale. Do not go beyond it to the point where content is hard to scan, and avoid all-caps headlines.

## 2. Two-column content layouts

- **What it is:** Title or label on one side, narrative content on the other.
- **Shown in:** Jonas, Formula Vintage, Kristian, Alex Graham services.
- **Why it is relevant:** It separates the statement from the explanation, and gives a calm reading rhythm.
- **Verdict:** **Adopt** as the default for case studies and capabilities.

## 3. Large project imagery with little chrome

- **What it is:** One big image, short copy, minimal containers.
- **Shown in:** Alex Graham Projects, Kristian, Formula Vintage, Julian.
- **Why it is relevant:** Imagery rules say the work itself is the hero.
- **Verdict:** **Adopt.**

## 4. Hairline-ruled information (tables and lists)

- **What it is:** Fact tables and service lists built with thin dividers.
- **Shown in:** Jonas, Kristian, Alex Graham services, Laura Gonzalez.
- **Why it is relevant:** It matches the brand preference for dividers and whitespace over containers.
- **Verdict:** **Adopt.**

## 5. Small label tier

- **What it is:** Tiny labels above or beside values.
- **Shown in:** Kristian, Jonas, Alex Graham, Catalin.
- **Why it is relevant:** It gives a third level of hierarchy without extra weight, and the design system already defines a label style.
- **Verdict:** **Adopt** using Inter. Do not use mono.

## 6. Strong, low-density hero

- **What it is:** One message, a portrait, a few facts.
- **Shown in:** All hero screenshots.
- **Why it is relevant:** It fits "one primary message per section".
- **Verdict:** **Adopt.**

## 7. Sequential or typographic motion

- **What it is:** Text revealed or moved in reading order.
- **Shown in:** Re-Birth, Julian intro, Alex Graham and Laura Gonzalez names *(notes)*.
- **Why it is relevant:** It supports "motion explains a relationship".
- **Verdict:** **Adapt.** Use it only when it reinforces reading order. Avoid continuous loops, keep all text readable at every step, and support reduced motion.

## 8. Dark backgrounds

- **What it is:** Near-black pages with white type.
- **Shown in:** Kristian, Catalin, Laura Gonzalez, Julian, Re-Birth, WhatIDo.
- **Why it is relevant:** It looks premium, but the Denizign default is a warm light background.
- **Verdict:** **Adapt.** Use dark only for deliberate sections, as the design system describes.

## 9. Selective accent on a phrase

- **What it is:** One phrase highlighted in colour or italic.
- **Shown in:** Kristian, Catalin, Alex Graham.
- **Why it is relevant:** The brand allows red on selected words.
- **Verdict:** **Adapt.** One short phrase, never a whole paragraph.

## 10. Portrait photography interacting with type

- **What it is:** The name and the portrait share the composition.
- **Shown in:** Alex Graham, Laura Gonzalez, Julian, Kristian.
- **Why it is relevant:** It adds personality, per the imagery rules.
- **Verdict:** **Adapt.** Keep contrast at AA wherever text overlaps an image.

# How this translates to Denizign

1. Use bold, oversized Manrope typography as the primary visual element, within the defined type scale.
2. Use restrained Inter typography for supporting content, in a narrow reading column.
3. Make the hero one dominant message, with a portrait and a small row of facts (location, focus, availability) in the label tier.
4. Prefer editorial two-column layouts for project explanations and capabilities.
5. Let large project imagery carry more visual weight than containers or cards.
6. Open every case study with a title and a ruled fact row (role, client, year, scope), followed immediately by large imagery.
7. Structure case studies into clearly separated narrative sections: context, complexity, role, thinking, intervention, outcome and reflection.
8. Present capabilities as structured lists with hairline dividers, grouped by layer, instead of chips or tiles.
9. Use generous whitespace to separate narrative moments, and keep case-study body copy quieter than hero typography.
10. Use scroll-driven typography only when it reinforces reading order, and sequence reveals deliberately, never randomly.
11. Keep dark sections rare and purposeful, and keep the default page light.
12. Keep red a small, deliberate accent: one phrase, one action or one detail at a time.

# Brand consistency check

Compared against `brand-foundation.md`, `colors.md`, `typography.md`, `imagery.md`, `motion.md` and the design system.

| Inspiration pattern | Conflict | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dark, near-black pages (most references) | Default background is warm white; dark is for punctuation only | **Adapt.** Dark sections sparingly |
| Serif display type (Kristian) | Only Manrope and Inter are approved | **Reject** |
| Mono labels and `//` prefixes (Julian, Alex Graham) | An extra typeface | **Reject.** Use the Inter label style |
| Gradients and saturated colour backdrops (Kristian, Julian, Jonas) | No gradients; restrained saturation | **Reject** |
| Teal accent and glowing chips (Laura Gonzalez) | Red is the only accent; no glow | **Reject** |
| Pill-shaped images and buttons (Catalin, Julian, Kristian) | Pills only for tags and statuses | **Reject** |
| Starfield particles and custom cursors | Decorative, no narrative meaning | **Reject** |
| Name animation looping horizontally (Alex Graham, Laura Gonzalez) | Brand motion avoids continuous looping | **Adapt.** One-time or scroll-linked movement |
| Text overlaid on a face | Risk to contrast and accessibility | **Adapt.** Keep AA contrast; overlap only where the text stays legible |
| Dimmed, unhighlighted text (Re-Birth, Julian) | Low contrast would fail AA if it is real content | **Adapt.** Keep dimmed text readable, and fully visible under reduced motion |
| Whole sentences in accent colour (Alex Graham intro) | Breaks the 10% red guideline | **Adapt.** One phrase only |
| All-caps display headlines (Catalin) | Not in the type scale | **Reject** |
| Phone mockups as default (Kristian) | Imagery rules avoid excessive device mockups | **Adapt.** Show UI large, mockups only when they help |
| Outlined giant numerals (Alex Graham) | Possibly decorative | **Adapt.** Use only if it aids navigation of a numbered list |
| Template widgets and badges (Framer, "Honors") | Not part of any design | **Ignore** |

None of these conflicts requires a change to the brand or the design system.

# Explicitly avoid

The final direction should NOT encourage:

- gradients
- colourful SaaS aesthetics
- glassmorphism
- excessive cards
- generic AI visuals
- excessive border radius
- decorative 3D without narrative meaning
- excessive animation
- copied Framer templates
- portfolios where visual effects overpower the actual UX work
- animations that reduce readability
- typography so large that content becomes difficult to scan
